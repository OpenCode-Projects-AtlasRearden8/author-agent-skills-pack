# Example: Chapter-by-Chapter Time Table

A second skill run on the same book, focused only on timeline. Compress every dated or timed event into one table, in reading order. Gaps and overlaps become obvious once the story is flattened into rows.

```markdown
# Timeline Table — *Cold Channel*

| Chapter | Story day | Date stated   | Event                                    | Character ages     |
|---------|-----------|---------------|------------------------------------------|--------------------|
| 1       | 1         | (none)        | Body found at Harbor Pier                | —                  |
| 2       | 1         | (none)        | Marla interviews deckhand                 | Marcus: "early 50s"|
| 3       | 2         | flashback 1996| Ferry runs aground (summer)              | Marcus: 12         |
| 4       | 3         | (none)        | Marcus recounts accident                  | —                  |
| 5       | 4         | (none)        | Boat trip past Harbor Pier                | —                  |

## Flags
- Ch. 3 flash back to 1996 with Marcus aged 12 → birth year ≈ 1984 → age in 2024 ≈ 40.
  Ch. 2 says "early 50s". CONFLICT (certain) — same as finding #1.
- Story days 1–4 are consistent; no impossible travel times found.
```

Tips:

- One row per event, even tiny ones; merging rows hides gaps.
- Use `(none)` for scenes without a stated date rather than guessing one.
- Ages always get their own column — age arithmetic is where most timeline errors hide.
- Keep this table in `consistency/timeline.md`; it doubles as a reference for later books in the series.
